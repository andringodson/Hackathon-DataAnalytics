Growth hid two fixable failures.

VoltRelay tripled its revenue in 18 months, but service failures grew almost six-fold and every swap still loses money after battery wear. The cause is not competition or demand. It is overheating Gen1 cabinets and three bad battery lots.

01

Network performance over time

Four metrics, four different stories

Volume and revenue grew steadily. Failures spiked every summer, and contribution per swap after battery wear barely moved, because rising wear cancelled out the July 2024 price rise.

02

Service failures and customer experience

Failure is concentrated, seasonal and predictable

All 25 worst stations are Gen1 launch sites in three cities. The experience breaks through empty cabinets, not long queues: queue wait is ~4 minutes everywhere.

03

Station and geographic patterns

Gen1 cabinets cannot charge in the heat

Hourly telemetry shows the mechanism. Controlling for city, site type, host and connectivity, a Gen1 cabinet on a hot day fails 9 pp more often than a Gen2 cabinet on the same day. Location type and connectivity barely matter.

Expansion went where sites were easy, not where riders were failing

Wave 1 added 30 stations, mostly highway fuel pumps and residential sites running ~55 swaps a day, against ~77 at launch hubs. New stations show almost no relationship with where service was failing (r = 0.11). The Gen1 hubs behind the failures were not upgraded, and their summer failure rate in 2025 was unchanged from 2024.

04

Battery and equipment performance

Three bad lots drove margin erosion and falling range

Kyron's first deliveries (Jul–Sep 2024) lose state of health about three times faster than every other lot, including Kyron's own later lots. That points to a batch defect, not a bad supplier.

05

Pricing and partner economics

Pricing helped revenue, not reliability

The July 2024 price rise had no visible demand penalty. The peak pilot shifted ~3 pp of demand and added ₹6–8 per exposed swap, but it ran in the two least congested cities, so reliability did not change.

06

Root cause of new-rider churn

New riders leave when the network fails them early

Monthly cohorts' retention moves almost perfectly against their early failure rate (r = −0.87). A logistic model and a hold-out gradient-boosting check both rank early failures and delivered range as the primary drivers.

Primary

Early service failure and delivered range in the first 14 days

Secondary

3W vehicle · hot, Gen1-heavy home city · partner-billed plan · peak-price exposure (minor)

Not drivers

Competitor nearby · competitor promos · signup channel · KYC
